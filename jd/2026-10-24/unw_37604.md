Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security. Placing women’s rights at the centre of all its efforts, the UN Women leads and coordinates the United Nations system efforts to ensure that commitments on gender equality and gender mainstreaming translate into action throughout the world. It provides strong and coherent leadership in support of Member States’ priorities and efforts, building effective partnerships with civil society and other relevant actors.

UN Women’s triple mandate, along with its global network and deep policy and programming expertise, continues to endow the Entity with a unique capacity to: (i) support Member States to strengthen global norms and standards for gender equality and women’s empowerment, and mainstream gender perspectives in other thematic areas; (ii) promote coordination and coherence across the UN system to enhance accountability and results for gender equality and women’s empowerment; and (iii) undertake operational activities to support Member States, upon their request, in translating global norms and standards into legislation, policies and strategies at country level.

UN Women plays an innovative and catalytic role in the State of Palestine since its inception in 1997 (as UNIFEM). In line with the national priorities, the work of UN Women for the period 2023-2025 is aligned with three of the Palestine United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework’s (UNSDCF) outcomes:

• Palestinians have greater access to economic opportunities that are inclusive, resilient, and sustainable, including decent employment and livelihoods opportunities in an empowered private sector.

• Palestinians, including the most vulnerable, have equal access to sustainable, inclusive, gender responsive and quality social services, social protection, and affordable utilities.

• Palestinian governance institutions, processes, and mechanisms at all levels are more democratic, rights-based, inclusive, and accountable.

Its three-year Strategic Note supports the efforts to achieve measurable results in country in terms of four UN Women global Strategic Plan impacts (2022-2025): (SP Impact 1). Governance and participation in public life; (SP Impact 2). Women’s Economic Empowerment; (SP Impact 3). Ending Violence Against Women and Girls; SP Impact 4. Women peace and security, Humanitarian & Disaster Risk Reduction.

In crisis and recovery contexts, women and girls often experience a disproportionate increase in unpaid and underpaid care responsibilities due to displacement, destruction of services, loss of livelihoods, disability, injury, and increased household needs. These care burdens often limit women's ability to engage in education, skills development, employment, entrepreneurship and recovery processes.

Under several recovery initiatives in Gaza, including programmes focused on women's economic recovery, employment generation, enterprise rehabilitation, disability inclusion and livelihood restoration, UN Women is supporting women-headed households, caregivers, women with disabilities and other vulnerable groups to access economic opportunities. These programmes identify unpaid care responsibilities as one of the most significant barriers preventing women's participation and retention in employment, skills development and enterprise recovery pathways.

At the same time, the care economy itself presents important opportunities for employment creation, livelihood generation and resilience-building. Strengthening community-based care systems, supporting paid caregiving services, and integrating care solutions into recovery programming can simultaneously reduce barriers to women's economic participation while creating new jobs and enterprise opportunities.

UN Women seeks a consultant to provide technical expertise on care economy programming, caregiving systems, unpaid care and domestic work, and gender-responsive recovery programming. The consultant will support the design, implementation, evidence generation and institutionalization of care-focused approaches across UN Women's recovery portfolio in Gaza.

The consultant will report to the UN Women Programme Analyst (Humanitarian and Early recovery) and work closely with UN Women programme staff, partners, national stakeholders and Joint Programme partners.

Description of Responsibilities /Scope of Work

1. Strengthen Women's Economic Empowerment and Care-Responsive Programming

- Lead technical efforts to surge women’s employment during Gaza’s early recovery.

- Guide the integration of care-responsive approaches across humanitarian, recovery and development programmes.

- Identify and address care-related barriers affecting women's participation in employment, livelihoods, skills development and entrepreneurship.

- Develop practical recommendations on childcare, disability care, caregiving support, referral systems and enabling services including community based care models, workplace care, and formal care organizations.

- Advise on the establishment of informal and formal care structures in crisis settings including: Safeguarding, PSEA and protection systems including child protection and protection for vulnerable groups; SOPs; Decent work standards for care workers; Accountability and complaints mechanisms; Quality assurance, monitoring and supervision; Childcare and care infrastructure investments; transitioning from informal community based care to formal models of care.

- Guide the design of gender-responsive care interventions for vulnerable groups, including women with disabilities, caregivers and women-headed households.

2. Advance Care Economy Approaches and Evidence

- Conduct and lead care economy assessments, care needs analyses and labour market assessments related to care services.

- Identify opportunities for creating decent jobs and enterprise opportunities within the care economy.

- Develop evidence, policy recommendations and practical guidance on care systems, care services and women's economic empowerment.

- Guide the collection and analysis of sex-, age- and disability-disaggregated data related to care work and care needs.

3. Strengthen Capacity, Partnerships and Learning

- Design and deliver capacity-building initiatives on care-responsive programming and care economy approaches.

- Strengthen engagement with WLOs, service providers, government institutions, private sector actors and development partners.

- Facilitate dialogue and partnerships around care systems, care needs, and care-related employment.

- Develop tools, guidance products and learning materials to support scaling up care-responsive and care-focused programmes including transitioning from informal community care models into more formal care structures including work place care and private sector.
