Org. Setting and Reporting
The Office of Military Affairs provides military expertise and advice to senior United Nations leadership in support of the planning, deployment and effective functioning of military components in United Nations peace operations. As mission transitions accelerate and political dynamics continue to evolve across multiple theatres, OMA requires a strong political affairs capability to ensure that operational assessments, planning processes and strategic advice are informed by appropriate political analysis. The Senior Political Affairs Officer provides political analysis and strategic advice to the Military Adviser on developments affecting United Nations peace operations, with particular attention to mission transitions and related policy and operational considerations. The incumbent monitors and analyses political and peace and security developments, identifies emerging trends and their implications for United Nations military operations, and supports the formulation of coherent advice for senior leadership. This position is located in the Office of Military Affairs (OMA), Department of Peace Operations (DPO), at United Nations Headquarters in New York. The incumbent reports directly to the Military Adviser, Assistant Secretary-General (ASG).

Responsibilities
Within delegated authority, the Senior Political Affairs Officer will be responsible for the following duties: • Monitor, research and analyse political, peace and security developments and processes in assigned geographic and/or thematic areas, identifying emerging issues, trends and potential risks; assess their implications for United Nations peace operations, mission transitions, policy formulation, strategic planning and decision-making, including through the use of digital technologies and data analytics. • Oversee the preparation of analytical and strategic documentation, including reports, briefing notes, talking points, concept papers, policy papers and correspondence for the Military Adviser and other senior United Nations officials, to support informed decision-making on peace and security, mission transitions and related policy and operational matters. • Develop strategies, policy options, plans and initiatives related to peacekeeping, early warning, conflict prevention, disarmament, mediation, conflict resolution, peacebuilding and mission transitions, including recommendations on immediate and longer-term approaches to evolving political and operational challenges. • Advise on the political implications of operational assessments, military planning and proposed peacekeeping initiatives, including the development or review of policies, guidance, terms of reference, operational concepts, plans and timelines for new, transitioning or reconfigured operations. • Coordinate complex programmes, projects, missions and other activities requiring engagement across departments, agencies and different components of United Nations operations, including civilian, military and humanitarian actors; ensure coherent responses to comments, statements, queries and emerging issues requiring cross-cutting analysis. • Organize programmatic and strategic activities in the assigned area of work, including internal and inter-agency coordination mechanisms, field and support missions, consultations, workshops, conferences and other engagements at United Nations Headquarters. • Provide substantive backstopping and political affairs support to United Nations peace operations and offices away from Headquarters, including analysis and advice on major political developments, mission transitions, reconfiguration, drawdown and other developments affecting the implementation of mandates. • Guide support, as required, to intergovernmental processes and bodies, including the Security Council, General Assembly and their subsidiary bodies, through the preparation of substantive inputs, advice on relevant political and operational matters and coordination of activities arising from their deliberations and decisions. • Establish and maintain effective working relationships with senior government officials, Permanent Missions of Member States, United Nations partners, international and regional organizations, host governments, civil society, academia and other stakeholders, and provide advice on peace and security matters of mutual concern. • Represent the Office of Military Affairs and, as appropriate, the United Nations in meetings, consultations, working groups, conferences, seminars and other fora dealing with political, peacekeeping and international peace and security matters. •Promote organizational learning and institutional knowledge by facilitating the collection, analysis and dissemination of lessons learned, good practices and relevant policy and operational experience, particularly in relation to peace operations and mission transitions. •Direct the development, review and dissemination of policies, guidance and related strategic products to ensure consistency with evolving political conditions, mandated priorities and operational requirements. •Ensure that relevant rights-based approaches, conflict-prevention considerations, gender/Women, Peace and Security (WPS), Youth, Peace and Security (YPS), environmental and/or climate security perspectives are appropriately incorporated into political analysis, policy advice, planning and operational recommendations. •Supervise the substantive work of assigned personnel, as applicable, including setting priorities, allocating work, providing guidance and ensuring the quality and timely completion of outputs. •Perform other duties as assigned.
