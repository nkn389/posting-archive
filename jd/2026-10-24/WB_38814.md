South Asia Region Context

The South Asia Region, comprising Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, and Sri Lanka, is at a pivotal stage of development. Rapid economic growth, driven by urbanization and demographic trends, has reduced poverty, but significant disparities persist. The region also faces growing climate risks, environmental pollution, informality, and low female labor force participation.

Sustained, private sector-led growth, anchored in investments in infrastructure, logistics, energy, and connectivity, is critical to creating jobs for South Asia’s sizeable youth population. At the same time, continued investment in human capital is needed to equip people for the jobs of the future.

The South Asia Infrastructure program supports countries in building sustainable, resilient, and inclusive infrastructure systems through an integrated, cross-sectoral approach to growth, job creation, connectivity, resilience, and service delivery. Infrastructure engagements increasingly combine public and private sector solutions, including collaboration across the World Bank Group, to maximize development impact.

Bhutan’s mountainous terrain and dispersed settlements make safe, reliable, and climate-resilient transport connectivity central to access to markets, jobs, public services, and regional opportunities. The transport agenda therefore spans road corridor development, asset management, road safety, trade and transit facilitation, regional connectivity, rural and urban accessibility, and institutional capacity.

Transport and Logistics Practice:

The Transport and Logistics Practice supports efficient, safe, resilient, and low-carbon connectivity across road, rail, urban transport, maritime transport, inland waterways, and aviation. Working with public and private sector partners, it combines investment and policy support to improve access, trade, economic integration, and the performance of transport services and supply chains.

In Bhutan, the Practice’s program focuses on road corridors, regional connectivity and trade facilitation, while also including urban mobility and e-mobility engagements in the pipeline. The program brings together lending, analytics, technical assistance, policy dialogue, and close engagement with the Royal Government of Bhutan and development partners, with attention to climate resilience, road safety, institutional strengthening, and sustainable financing.

About the Position:

ISAT1 seeks to recruit a Transport Specialist (GF), based in Thimphu, to support the preparation and implementation of transport operations and analytical and advisory work in Bhutan and, as needed, elsewhere in South Asia. While the position will contribute across the transport sector, its primary focus will be on road corridors, regional connectivity, trade and transport facilitation, resilient infrastructure, road safety, and institutional strengthening.

The selected candidate will serve as the principal in-country Transport and Logistics Practice representative in Bhutan and a key interface between government counterparts and task teams based elsewhere. Working closely with the Bhutan Country Office, the Country Management Unit, other sector colleagues, World Bank Group teams, development partners, consultants, contractors, and other stakeholders, the Specialist will represent transport in day-to-day country dialogue, maintain close awareness of implementation developments, and ensure timely coordination and follow-up. The position requires technical breadth, operational capability, strong local knowledge, sound judgment, and the ability to move effectively between field-level implementation issues and broader policy and program dialogue.

Duties and Responsibilities:

The Transport Specialist will perform a full range of technical, operational, analytical, and coordination functions, including but not limited to the following:

1. Operations and Portfolio Delivery

• Support and, as appropriate, lead or co-lead the identification, preparation, appraisal, and implementation of transport lending and non-lending activities in Bhutan.
• Contribute to project design, results frameworks, implementation arrangements, cost and economic analysis, risk assessment, procurement planning, and implementation support.
• Monitor implementation progress, results, risks, disbursements, contracts, and agreed actions, and prepare concise and decision-useful reporting for task teams, the Country Management Unit, and management.
• Help resolve implementation bottlenecks and provide practical advice on technical, contractual, procurement, institutional, environmental, social, and operational issues.
• Participate in missions and site visits, maintain regular follow-up with counterparts, and support timely completion and documentation of agreed actions.
• Coordinate with environmental, social, procurement, financial management, legal, and other specialists to ensure integrated and high-quality project delivery.

2. Sector Technical and Policy Support

• Provide technical and operational support across Bhutan’s transport program, including road corridors, regional connectivity, trade and transport facilitation, road asset management, road safety, urban mobility, public transport, pedestrian accessibility, and e-mobility.
• Contribute to the planning, design, construction, upgrading, operation, and maintenance of transport infrastructure and services, taking account of Bhutan’s mountainous terrain and implementation capacity.
• Support climate-resilient design, slope and drainage management, disaster-risk reduction, lifecycle asset management, construction quality, contract management, and maintenance performance.
• Assess corridor performance, accessibility, mobility, logistics, and service-delivery constraints using appropriate engineering evidence, economic analysis, data, geospatial tools, and stakeholder engagement.
• Assess opportunities for private sector participation, innovative and sustainable financing, digital solutions, and World Bank Group collaboration where these can improve transport outcomes.
• Integrate climate mitigation and resilience, gender, universal accessibility, social inclusion, environmental sustainability, road safety, and jobs considerations into transport strategies and investments.

• Serve as the principal day-to-day in-country interface for the Transport and Logistics Practice, maintaining effective relationships with relevant ministries, agencies, local authorities, the Country Management Unit, and other sector colleagues.
