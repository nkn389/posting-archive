BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE OF THE ASSIGNMENT:
WFP South Sudan operates in a conflict-affected and fragile environment where insecurity, displacement, climatic shocks and economic instability create overlapping needs and disrupt access, markets and assistance. Constrained resources require sharper prioritization and a stronger understanding of the people WFP serves. Across humanitarian food assistance, nutrition, school feeding and food systems programmes, identity management and delivery must connect programme choices with safe, inclusive and accountable assistance.
The Country Office Identity Management and Delivery Team is a strategic programme function, bringing together beneficiary knowledge, identity management, cash and voucher programming, digital innovation and delivery assurance across cash and in-kind assistance. Its role extends beyond processing registrations and transfers: it enables the Country Office to understand who is being assisted, adapt support to different population groups, connect interventions and strengthen programme quality, efficiency and transparency.
The assignment will lead IDM and Delivery as an integrated programme portfolio aligned with Country Strategic Plan priorities and the Country Office’s shift towards integrated, area-based programming. Priorities include strengthening beneficiary analysis and profiling, advancing SCOPE 2.0 for in-kind assistance, expanding appropriate digital delivery options, and embedding assurance throughout programme design and implementation. Decisions must reflect location-specific access, market and service-provider conditions, including liquidity, connectivity and barriers to identification and financial access.
The Team Lead will provide strategic advice to Programme management, lead the team’s workplan and change agenda, and bring together programme teams, Field Offices and enabling functions to deliver results. The role combines specialist programme leadership, team management and cross-functional coordination within delegated authority. It will advance practical, sustainable improvements while safeguarding humanitarian principles, personal data and affected people’s rights and choices.

KEY ACCOUNTABILITIES / RESPONSIBILITIES

- Lead the IDM and Delivery programme portfolio. Define and implement a coherent country-level strategy, prioritized workplan and results framework linking identity management, beneficiary knowledge, cash and voucher programming and digital delivery to Country Strategic Plan (CSP) outcomes. Advise Programme management on strategic choices, investment priorities and resource requirements, positioning the portfolio as a driver of integrated programming rather than a transactional service.
- Lead identity management and KYC analysis to inform programme decisions. Establish a stronger understanding of WFP beneficiaries through Know Your Customer (KYC) analysis, including vulnerability, demographic and socioeconomic profiling. Work with VAM, monitoring, protection and programme teams to translate responsibly managed beneficiary data into evidence for targeting, prioritization, differentiated assistance and the layering and sequencing of interventions. Maintain strategic oversight of identity-data quality and SCOPE (digital assistance platform) use across cash and in-kind programmes, while distinguishing programme profiling from financial-service-provider KYC requirements.
- Advance digital innovation and inclusive delivery solutions. Lead a context-sensitive roadmap for expanding mobile money, e-vouchers and innovative IDM solutions where feasible and appropriate. Build the programme case for investment and scale-up with TEC, Finance, Supply Chain and service providers, using evidence on user experience, costs, reliability and protection risks. Address barriers related to identification, connectivity, digital literacy, liquidity and safe access, retaining suitable alternatives for people who cannot use digital channels.
- Lead portfolio assurance, accountability and responsible data use. Embed risk-based assurance in portfolio planning and delivery, driving follow-up to the augmented reassurance action plan and relevant oversight recommendations. Set expectations for identity integrity, traceability, segregation of duties and timely resolution of exceptions without duplicating independent assurance functions. Use management evidence and community feedback to address exclusion, diversion and delivery risks, strengthen transparency and ensure that data protection and accountability to affected populations inform decisions.
- Shape integrated and adaptive assistance delivery. Guide delivery-model and modality choices across programme portfolios in partnership with Field Offices and functional teams. Connect beneficiary evidence, market analysis and operational feasibility to programme design, including emergency preparedness and continuity arrangements for conflict, displacement and seasonal disruption. Provide oversight of delivery performance, service-provider effectiveness and portfolio resources, ensuring that technical processes and controls support timely, quality assistance and value for money.
- Build strategic partnerships and cross-functional alignment. Represent the IDM and Delivery portfolio in relevant country-level and inter-agency forums within delegated authority. Develop collaboration with government counterparts, UN agencies, cooperating partners, financial service providers and mobile operators to improve inclusive access, responsible interoperability and coordinated assistance. Draw on experience from other WFP operations.
- Lead and develop a results-oriented team. Translate portfolio priorities into clear roles, delegated responsibilities and accountable workplans across IDM and delivery workstreams. Supervise, coach and develop team members, strengthen national capacity and provide coherent technical support to Field Offices and partners. Foster inclusion, collaboration and staff wellbeing; manage competing demands with sound judgement and resilience; and lead change while escalating strategic risks and decisions to Programme management.

Advanced university degree in International Development, Public Policy, Economics, Social Sciences, Statistics, Information Management, Computer Science, Engineering or another field relevant to programme management, identity management and digital assistance; or a first university degree in a relevant field with two additional years of related professional experience.

Experience:
A minimum of five years of relevant postgraduate professional experience with an advanced university degree, or seven years with a first university degree, with substantive responsibility in one or more of the following areas: identity management, beneficiary data and analysis, cash and voucher programming or digital assistance delivery substantive responsibility for identity management, beneficiary data and analysis, cash and voucher programming or digital assistance delivery.

Demonstrated experience leading and managing teams, coordinating cross-functional work and translating strategy into programme results is required.
Experience in South Sudan or comparable complex operations, and familiarity with WFP programme systems and assurance requirements, are assets.

Knowledge & Skills:

- Strategic thinking and programme-portfolio management: ability to connect beneficiary evidence, operational choices, resources and results, and provide clear, actionable advice to management.
- Strong team leadership and people-management skills, including delegation, coaching, capacity development, inclusive working practices and the ability to lead change across technical and programme teams.
- Sound knowledge of IDM, beneficiary-data governance and analysis, including vulnerability, demographic and socioeconomic profiling, and the ability to translate analysis into programme decisions with appropriate data-protection safeguards.
- Ability to guide mobile money, e-voucher and innovative IDM initiatives from programme design through testing, learning and responsible scale-up, balancing accountability, efficiency, transparency and inclusion.
- Strong judgement in risk management and operational assurance, with the ability to identify strategic issues, oversee corrective action and maintain humanitarian principles under pressure.
- Resilience, adaptability and sound decision-making in insecure, rapidly changing and resource-constrained environments; strong communication, negotiation and partnership-building skills.
- Applied AI skills are desirable, particularly for analysis, knowledge management and identifying operational patterns, with an understanding of confidentiality, bias, validation and human oversight.
