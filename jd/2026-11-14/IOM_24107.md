Project Context and Scope
This consultancy contributes to the implementation of the “Central Asia Safe Migration” project funded by the UK Integrated Security Fund (ISF), which aims to reduce migrants’ vulnerability to exploitation, recruitment fraud, and unsafe migration while supporting sustainable reintegration for returning migrants across Central Asia. In the context of this consultancy, reintegration refers to the sustainable re-establishment of returning migrants in their communities through economic, social and psychosocial support measures that enable them to resume productive lives and contribute to national development. Effective reintegration extends beyond return itself and encompasses access to employment and livelihoods, recognition and utilization of skills acquired abroad, financial inclusion, social inclusion, and appropriate health and psychosocial support. As migration and return dynamics continue to evolve in Tajikistan, there is a growing need for evidence-based policies and coordinated programmes that address the diverse needs of returning migrants. IOM supports this objective through research, assessments, direct assistance, skills development and recognition, employment support, financial inclusion initiatives, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). It also promotes a positive narrative around migration, recognising the valuable skills, experience and networks that migrants bring back to Tajikistan.
In this context, the National Consultant will provide a comprehensive assessment of existing frameworks, institutional arrangements, stakeholder roles, service delivery capacities and gaps in support, while identifying opportunities to strengthen sustainable reintegration outcomes through enhanced coordination and engagement with public institutions, civil society and the private sector. As a minimum, the assessment should cover:

- Relevant national institutions, policies, strategies, programmes, coordination mechanisms, and service delivery frameworks related to migrant return and reintegration in Tajikistan.

- Key government and non-government stakeholders involved in reintegration planning and service provision, including institutions responsible for labour migration, employment, social protection, vocational education and training, local government, financial inclusion, health services, civil society, private sector engagement and international development partners.

- Existing reintegration programmes, referral pathways and support services available to returning migrants, including employment assistance, skills recognition and certification, entrepreneurship support, social assistance, psychosocial support and community-based reintegration initiatives, with particular attention to their accessibility, eligibility requirements, institutional coverage, available capacities, implementation experience and evidence of results.

- A review of available research, policy documents, programme evaluations, administrative data and other relevant evidence related to migrant return, reintegration outcomes and the needs of returnees.

- An assessment of institutional capacities, coordination mechanisms and service delivery systems to identify strengths, gaps, overlaps and opportunities for strengthening sustainable reintegration support at national and local levels, including potential institutional arrangements for coordinating, implementing and monitoring the future National Reintegration Plan, building on existing government structures and interministerial coordination mechanisms.

- Consultations with relevant government agencies, development partners, civil society organisations, private sector representatives, training institutions and migrant associations.

- Analysis of the economic, social and psychosocial needs of returning migrants as conveyed by stakeholder perspectives, including barriers to labour market reintegration, access to services, skills utilization, financial inclusion and social inclusion, as well as opportunities to strengthen employment facilitation, recognition of skills acquired abroad, entrepreneurship support, access to financial services and engagement with private-sector employers and service providers, with consideration of the specific needs of women, youth and other vulnerable groups.

In summary, the assessment is intended to provide a comprehensive overview of Tajikistan’s reintegration ecosystem, identifying existing capacities, service delivery gaps and priority areas for intervention which will then help to inform the development of an evidence-based, coordinated, and sustainable National Reintegration Plan.
