Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security.

The UN Women Knowledge and Partnerships Centre in Seoul operates as a specialized regional applied knowledge hub under the strategic oversight of the Regional Office for Asia and the Pacific (ROAP). In Phase 2 (2027-2032), the Centre bridges UN Women’s expertise with the Republic of Korea’s policy innovations, digital governance, and networks. It delivers adaptable knowledge products and tools, validated through Regional Technical Policy Labs and deployed via ROAP and Country Offices, focused on Women’s Economic Empowerment (WEE) and Ending Violence Against Women (EVAW).

The TFVAW workstream explicitly addresses structural barriers to safety and justice shaped by rapid technological transitions, artificial intelligence, and evolving online harms. It focuses specifically on strengthening knowledge, regional response strategies, policies, and tools to combat Technology Facilitated Violence Against Women (TFVAW), non-consensual image manipulation, and digital platform safety gaps. Drawing on Korea's advanced digital infrastructure, legislative frameworks, and public safety mechanisms, the workstream creates regional resources. The Centre collaborates closely with the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family (MOGEF) as its primary partner, while mobilizing public, private, tech-sector, academic, and civil society networks.

The Policy Specialist, EVAW, reports to the Centre Director for daily operations and performance management and maintains a functional technical line to the ROAP EVAW lead to ensure corporate alignment and coherence. Under the overall guidance of the Centre Director and in coordination with the ROAP EVAW lead, the Policy Specialist provides technical expertise and coordinates the implementation of the Centre's EVAW initiatives and related knowledge products.

Key Functions and Accountabilities:

1. Design and implement the Centre EVAW initiatives and develop specialized knowledge products

- Formulate, research, and author comparative regional papers, briefs, and toolkits addressing digital care systems and gender bias in AI-driven labor frameworks;

- Identify, evaluate, and package promising practices and employer standards, using Korea’s policy and technological experiences as a core reference point;

- Manage and clear technical review cycles with HQ and ROAP teams to anchor the Centre's digital and AI expertise within corporate normative frameworks.

2. Manage the Centre’s regional validation initiatives, expert exchange, and technical peer learning

- Coordinate the substantive design and execution of the Centre’s Regional Technical Policy Labs, expert consultations, and Communities of Practice to validate framework models;

- Consolidate and synthesize technical feedback from line ministries, algorithmic regulators, civil society, and tech innovators into regional guidance assets;

- Coordinate with ROAP and HQ thematic leads to ensure technical integrity and regional coherence.

3. Support the development and maintenance of strategic tech-ecosystem partnerships and contribute to workstream resource mobilization

- Establish and sustain strategic partnerships with MOGEF, key line ministries, and specialized national public agencies in the Republic of Korea;

- Identify and facilitate partnerships with premier Korean digital platforms, technology firms, research universities, and cybersecurity governance bodies;

- Support strategic outreach to Korea-based private sector entities and networks to promote the integration of digital safety and corporate accountability into technical architectures;

- Contribute to the development of tailored value propositions and resource mobilization proposals for national and regional donors and tech-sector partners to mobilize financial or in-kind resources.

4. Provide technical advisory support to enable and facilitate tool uptake by field-based presences

- Provide specialized technical advice and tools to Country Office teams to support their integration of the Centre’s TFVAW and digital safety flagships into national policy dialogues in collaboration with ROAP;

- Advise and guide field presences upon request on localizing comparative resources to match varying national regulatory and digital realities;

- Maintain a continuous technical feedback loop to ensure field implementation insights directly drive the iterative upgrading of Centre tools.

5. Manage the implementation, budget pipelines, and personnel of the EVAW workstream within the Centre

- Prepare the annual workplan and budget for the EVAW workstream under the operational direction of the Centre Director;

- Coordinate technical implementation and monitor financial delivery rates under ROAP financial oversight;

- Supervise, mentor, and evaluate the performance of workstream personnel, short-term consultants, and technical researchers assigned to the pillar.

6. Lead advocacy, knowledge building, and communication efforts on digital gender equality and safety

- Represent the Centre as delegated in regional meetings and high-level policy dialogues on TFVAW, online safety standards, and digital platform accountability;

- Identify and serialize best practices and lessons learned to guide the continuous improvement of knowledge management strategies;

- Coordinate collaboration with the Centre’s communications team and ROAP to package complex findings into polished policy advocacy assets.

7. Perform other duties within their functional profile as deemed necessary for the efficient functioning of the Centre and the Organization.

Statements :

In July 2010, the United Nations General Assembly created UN Women, the United Nations Entity for Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women. The creation of UN Women came about as part of the UN reform agenda, bringing together resources and mandates for greater impact. It merges and builds on the important work of four previously distinct parts of the UN system (DAW, OSAGI, INSTRAW and UNIFEM), which focused exclusively on gender equality and women's empowerment.
