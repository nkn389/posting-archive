Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security.

UN Women works to advance gender equality and women’s economic empowerment by strengthening women’s access to productive assets, skills, finance, technology and markets. In Cross River State, the cassava value chain offers significant opportunities for rural women to increase productivity, diversify products, reduce post-harvest losses and build sustainable enterprises. This assignment supports the planned UN Women partnership for rural women in the cassava value chain in Cross River State, including the initiative being developed with the MTN Foundation. The intervention is intended to translate access to processing technologies into measurable improvements in women’s technical competence, product quality, enterprise management, and market readiness.

Women are active across cassava production and processing but may face constraints including limited access to improved processing techniques, inconsistent product quality, weak packaging and branding, occupational health and safety risks, restricted access to finance and markets, and the time burden associated with unpaid care work. UN Women therefore seeks qualified local trainer(s) with practical cassava-processing and adult-learning experience to provide hands-on, gender-responsive training to selected women beneficiaries in Cross River State.

The trainer will work under the overall guidance of the UN Women Nigeria Country Office and the direct supervision of Women’s Economic Empowerment Programme Specialist. The trainer will coordinate with relevant implementing partners, beneficiary groups and local stakeholders in Cross River State. All deliverables must be submitted physical and electronically in editable formats and are subject to review and acceptance by UN Women.

The assignment will be implemented in selected project communities'/processing locations in Cross River State mainly in Obubra. The final training venue, beneficiary numbers and logistics will be confirmed by UN Women. Any authorized travel must follow the contractual arrangement and applicable UN Women procedures. The trainer should not assume that transport, accommodation, training equipment or consumables are reimbursable unless explicitly provided in the contract.

Purpose of the Assignment

The purpose of the assignment is to strengthen the practical, business and organizational capacities of women cassava processors so that they can operate processing equipment safely and efficiently, improve the quality and value of cassava products, reduce losses and production costs, meet relevant food-safety requirements, and improve their access to markets and income-generating opportunities.

Specific Objectives

- Train women on good cassava-processing practices from raw material selection through processing, storage and sale.

- Build practical skills for safe operation, routine cleaning, basic maintenance and responsible use of cassava-processing equipment.

- Improve product quality, hygiene, food safety, packaging, labelling, storage and traceability practices.

- Strengthen value addition for products appropriate to local demand, including gari, high-quality cassava flour, fufu/akpu and other viable derivatives where relevant.

- Strengthening enterprise skills in costing, pricing, record keeping, inventory management, savings, financial literacy and cooperative business management.

- Improve women’s understanding of aggregation, collective marketing, buyer engagement, quality specifications, and market linkages.

- Promote gender-responsive, climate-smart and environmentally responsible practices, including waste management and productive use of cassava residues where feasible.

Scope of Work and Responsibilities:

- Conduct a rapid inception assessment of participants’ existing skills, processing practices, equipment, product mix and priority learning needs.

- Prepare a concise, practical training plan and materials using accessible language, demonstrations, illustrations and locally relevant examples. Materials should be suitable for participants with different literacy levels.

- Deliver hands-on training on cassava reception and sorting; peeling, washing, grating, pressing/dewatering, fermentation where applicable, pulverizing/sieving, roasting or drying, milling, cooling, hygienic handling, packaging and storage.

- Demonstrate safe equipment start-up, operation, shutdown, cleaning, routine preventive maintenance, fault identification and escalation. Training must emphasize PPE, machine guarding, electrical/fuel safety and safe handling around moving parts.

- Train participants on good hygiene and manufacturing practices, contamination prevention, water quality, moisture control, storage, batch identification and quality assurance. Where products are sold as packaged foods, explain the practical relevance of applicable NAFDAC requirements and Standards Organisation of Nigeria specifications without presenting the training as regulatory certification.

- Facilitate modules on enterprise management, including calculation of processing costs and margins, simple bookkeeping, stock control, pricing, customer management, savings and responsible use of finance.

- Support participants to assess viable product and market opportunities, develop simple market-linkage actions, and improve packaging/branding decisions based on buyer requirements.

- Integrate gender equality throughout the training, including women’s control over productive assets and income, leadership within cooperatives, equitable participation in decision-making, safeguarding, and practical scheduling that recognizes unpaid care responsibilities.

- Administer pre- and post-training assessments and maintain sex-disaggregated attendance and participation records.

- Provide short post-training coaching/mentoring and submit a final report documenting results, participant feedback, lessons, photographs where consent is obtained, recommendations and outstanding technical support needs.

Training Methodology

The trainer will use an experiential and participant-centred approach, with approximately 70% of contact time devoted to practical demonstrations, supervised practice and problem solving. Methods should include demonstrations of equipment, small-group exercises, product-quality checks, business simulations, peer learning, question-and-answer sessions and coaching. The approach must be respectful, inclusive and adapted to the local context. Training dates and daily schedules should be agreed with participants to reduce barriers arising from care responsibilities and travel constraints.

Indicative Training Modules

- Cassava quality, raw-material selection and post-harvest loss reduction

- Processing workflow and improved techniques for major cassava products

- Safe operation, cleaning and basic preventive maintenance of processing equipment

- Food hygiene, quality assurance, storage and basic traceability

- Value addition, packaging, branding and product presentation

- Costing, pricing, bookkeeping, inventory and financial literacy

- Cooperative governance, leadership, collective processing and marketing

- Market requirements, buyer engagement and market-linkage planning

- Gender equality, safeguarding, occupational safety and care-responsive work organization

- Climate-smart processing, waste reduction and environmentally responsible disposal/utilization of residues

Performance Indicators

- Approved inception report, curriculum and training tools delivered on time.

- At least 90% of enrolled participants complete the agreed training programme, subject to the final beneficiary list.

- At least 80% of participants demonstrate improved knowledge or skills between pre- and post-training assessments or practical competency checks.

- At least 80% of participants demonstrate correct key steps for safe equipment operation, hygiene and product-quality control during practical sessions.

- Participant satisfaction rate of at least 80%, with feedback disaggregated where feasible.

- Final report provides credible evidence of results, challenges, lessons and actionable recommendations for continued coaching, equipment management and market linkage.

Expected Deliverables

Inception package- Rapid needs assessment, participant profile, detailed workplan, training curriculum/modules, training tools and pre-test.

Practical training delivered - Hands-on technical training completed; attendance, photographs with consent, practical exercises and equipment-safety demonstrations documented.

Enterprise and market-readiness training plus coaching - Business, cooperative management and market-linkage modules completed; post-training coaching and participant action plans documented.

Final report - Final report including pre/post results, beneficiary reach, challenges, lessons, recommendations, training materials and supporting evidence.
