PURPOSE OF THE POSITION
To provide strategic medical and technical leadership for an integrated portfolio covering polio eradication, immunization-system strengthening and the prevention, control, elimination and outbreak response of vaccine-preventable diseases. The incumbent advises senior national authorities and WHO leadership, directs WHO technical inputs, manages multidisciplinary teams and resources, and builds high-level partnerships to translate global and regional commitments into equitable, evidence-based and sustainable country results.
OBJECTIVES OF THE PROGRAMME
To eradicate poliomyelitis; reduce morbidity, mortality and inequities attributable to vaccine-preventable diseases; achieve regional and global disease-control and elimination targets; and strengthen resilient national immunization and surveillance systems across the life course. The programme advances the Immunization Agenda 2030 and applicable Global Polio Eradication Initiative strategies through high and equitable routine immunization, quality supplementary immunization activities, timely introduction of vaccines, effective surveillance, and rapid preparedness and response to outbreaks and health emergencies.
DESCRIPTION OF DUTIES

- Provide strategic vision, authoritative technical advice and overall technical direction for integrated polio eradication, routine immunization, VPD surveillance, outbreak preparedness and response, and accelerated disease-control and elimination priorities.
- Lead the development, review and implementation of evidence-based policies, national and regional strategies, emergency action plans, immunization strategies, costed multi-year and annual plans, operational guidance and accountability frameworks aligned with WHO recommendations and Member State priorities.
- Oversee high-quality routine and supplementary immunization activities, including campaign optimization, reaching zero-dose and under-immunized populations, integration with primary health care, life-course vaccination, vaccine introduction and polio-transition planning.
- Direct preparedness, risk assessment and coordinated response to importations or outbreaks of wild or vaccine-derived poliovirus and other VPDs, including measles, rubella, diphtheria, meningitis, yellow fever and other priority diseases, in accordance with global and regional guidance.
- Ensure effective, integrated surveillance systems, including acute flaccid paralysis, environmental and case-based VPD surveillance and laboratory networks; establish performance standards and use epidemiological, immunization and programme data to guide senior decision-making and corrective action.
- Lead monitoring, evaluation, learning and programme reviews; establish key performance indicators, data-quality improvement strategies and accountability mechanisms, and ensure timely, accurate reporting to WHO governing, managerial and donor mechanisms.
- Manage the programme area and its human, financial and logistical resources; set team objectives, delegate authority, monitor performance, develop staff, manage risks and ensure efficient, compliant and timely implementation of workplans and grants.
- Build, negotiate and sustain strategic partnerships and coordination mechanisms with government, GPEI and immunization partners, donors, technical agencies, civil society and communities; resolve policy or operational differences and promote harmonized recommendations and action.
- Lead resource mobilization, grant development and stewardship, including investment cases, budgets, funding proposals, allocation decisions, timely utilization, risk management and quality donor reporting.
- Strengthen national decision-making and institutional capacity, including NITAGs, NCCs, NPECs and other advisory bodies, through policy dialogue, technical assistance, training, coaching and transfer of knowledge to national and subnational institutions.
- Promote and direct operational research, innovation and knowledge management on vaccines, immunization delivery, surveillance, disease eradication and outbreak reduction; translate evidence and lessons learned into WHO guidance, best practices and programme improvement.
- Serve as WHO's senior technical focal point and as necessary and guided represent the Organization in high-level national and international meetings, partner and donor forums, technical briefings and, when designated, public or media communications on polio, immunization and VPD priorities.
- Perform other related duties as assigned.
REQUIRED QULAIFICATINOS
Education
Essential: A university degree in medicine and a masters degree in public health, epidemiology, communicable or infectious diseases, vaccinology, global health, health policy or another field directly relevant to the functions of the position.
Desirable: Advanced professional training in epidemiology, communicable-disease control, immunization, vaccinology, outbreak management, health policy or programme leadership.
Experience
Essential: At least ten years of progressively responsible professional experience in public health, including at least seven years in immunization, polio eradication, VPD surveillance/control/elimination and/or outbreak preparedness and response. The experience must include responsibility for policy, strategy and/or programme performance. Relevant international experience gained outside the candidate's home country is mandatory.
Desirable: Experience in polio-endemic/high-risk, VPD outbreak-affected, low-performing immunization, fragile or humanitarian settings. Experience managing multidisciplinary teams and human and financial resources. Substantial work with national and international partners. Work with WHO, another UN organization, GPEI partner or major immunization partnerships; and demonstrated achievements in resource mobilization, donor stewardship, policy development, vaccine introduction, programme transition or large-scale campaign delivery.
Skills

- Detailed and comprehensive medical and public-health expertise in immunization systems, epidemiology, polio eradication, VPD prevention, control and elimination, surveillance, and outbreak preparedness and response; recognized ability to serve as an expert in one or more VPDs across organizational levels.
- Demonstrated ability to formulate strategic vision; develop and negotiate policies, strategies, standards and investment priorities; and convert them into feasible, measurable programmes in diverse and resource-constrained settings.
- Authoritative command of epidemiological, surveillance, immunization and programme data, including risk assessment, interpretation of complex evidence, and communication of clear recommendations to senior decision-makers.
- Proven leadership in managing and motivating diverse multidisciplinary teams and overseeing human, financial and operational resources, grants, risks and performance.
- Excellent diplomatic, partnership-building, negotiation, advocacy and resource-mobilization skills with governments, donors, UN agencies, technical partners, civil society and communities.
- Demonstrated capacity to lead programmes and coordinated response in fragile, conflict-affected, humanitarian or outbreak settings and to connect emergency action with resilient routine systems and primary health care.
- Excellent strategic communication, technical writing, public speaking and knowledge-transfer skills, with sound judgement in sensitive or high-profile situations.
- Commitment to WHO values, integrity, accountability, gender equality, equity, inclusion and respectful collaboration across cultures.
