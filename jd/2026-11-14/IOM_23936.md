Project Context and Scope
The International Organization for Migration (IOM) implements health program in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) to strengthen health system and improve access to quality health services, including for vulnerable, underserved, displaced, and mobile population.
The IOM project Resilient and Equitable Access to Care and Health in BASULTA (REACH-BASULTA), funded by Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), aims to enhance service utilization and improve the Maternal, Neonatal, and Child Health (MNCH) in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) and amongst mobile populations.

Under the project’s health system strengthening work, IOM supports the capacity development of health workers and frontline workers to provide inclusive, people-centered and culturally responsive MNCH service delivery is an important part of his work, particularly in communities affected by conflict, violence, disasters, displacement, and mobility.
Recognizing the role of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) in improving maternal and child health, the project supports its integration into MNCH services. This is particularly important in BARMM, where conflict, violence, disasters, and displacement may affect the psychosocial well-being of mothers, children, and families. To address gaps in health worker capacity and referral systems, IOM will strengthen providers’ ability to offer basic psychosocial support, recognize concerns, facilitate appropriate referrals, and deliver more integrated, inclusive MNCH-MHPSS care.
Therefore, this consultancy will develop and deliver practical training for at least 30 health workers and frontline workers from six health facilities on integrating culturally appropriate, migrant-sensitive MHPSS into MNCH services. It will also provide post-training mentoring aligned with the existing MOH-BARMM/JICA supportive supervision approach.
Under the overall guidance of the Head of Sub-Office in Cotabato City, direct supervision of the National Migration Health Project Officer for MNCH, the consultant will work in coordination with IOM technical staff and relevant DOH and MOH-BARMM counterparts.

Tasks to be performed under this contract
Consultancy is to develop, contextualize, and deliver a competency-based training programme on integrating MHPSS into MNCH services and applying migrant-sensitive approaches, followed by structured mentoring and follow-up support for the six health facilities.

A. FIRST DELIVERABLES: Inception, Document Review and Capacity Development Plan

- Submit an inception Report and detailed work plan outlining the methodology, key activities, timelines, coordination mechanisms, and quality assurance measures, and risks.
- Review relevant project assessments, policies, technical guidance, training materials, and reference documents related to MHPSS, MNCH, migration health and community-based service delivery.
- Prepare a concise analytical report identifying relevant capacity gaps, opportunities, operational considerations, and recommendations for integrating MHPSS and migration-sensitive approaches into MNCH services at the six health facilities.
- Prepare a capacity development plan for the six target facilities, identifying priority competencies, learning objectives, proposed training methods, follow-up needs, and suggested indicators.
- Consult with IOM and relevant DOH/MOH-BARMM counterparts to confirm the training scope, participants, facility context, and review process for the training materials.

Activities shall be completed with all required deliverable reports submitted by email to IOM no later than 30 December 2026 (30% of overall contract value), upon satisfactory completion and acceptance of the deliverable.
B. SECOND DELIVERABLES: Final the Integrated MHPSS-MNCH Training Package
The consultant shall review relevant national and international MHPSS, MNCH, and migrant health resources, including the DOH and BARMM Harmonized MHPSS Training Manual, the WHO Thinking Healthy guide, and Psychological First Aid (PFA) guidance. Based on this review and consultation with IOM and relevant stakeholders, the consultant shall develop a contextualized training package for frontline health workers.
The package shall address relevant MHPSS concepts and practical approaches for MNCH service delivery, including:
- Culturally grounded well-being and psychosocial support, including Mga Bilog ng Buhay and local coping practices;
- Maternal and perinatal psychosocial support, mother-infant interaction, and basic approaches adapted from Thinking Healthy;
- PFA, recognizing psychosocial distress, safe referral, and applicable consent and confidentiality considerations;
- Migrant-sensitive, gender-responsive, trauma-informed, human rights-based, and non-discriminatory care, including communication and continuity of care for mobile populations; and Staff well-being and “Caring for Carers.”
- The package shall incorporate relevant laws, policies, referral pathways, and ethical guidance, as validated with IOM and relevant counterparts. Content on risk identification and referral shall be appropriate to participants’ professional roles and consistent with approved protocols.
The training package shall include:
- Curriculum, agenda, and session plans;
- Facilitator’s guide and participant materials;
- PowerPoint presentations and other learning materials;
- Case studies, practical exercises, role plays, and simulations;
- Pre- and post-training assessment and evaluation tools; and
- Job aids and workflow integration tools to support application within routine MNCH services.
Activities shall be completed with all required deliverable reports submitted by email to IOM no later than 30 January 2027 (30% of overall contract value), upon satisfactory completion and acceptance of the deliverable.
C. THIRD DELIVERABLES: Training Delivery, Facility Follow-Up and Final Report

- Facilitate interactive, 4-to-5-day competency-based training for at least 30 health workers and frontline workers, including MHPSS focal points, from the six target health facilities. The training duration and schedule shall be agreed with IOM and relevant counterparts.
- Design a post-training mentoring and follow-up framework for the six target facilities to reinforce learning application, strengthen service delivery competencies, and support the institutionalization of MHPSS and migrant-sensitive approaches within routine MNCH services. The framework shall define mentoring objectives, roles and responsibilities, monitoring mechanisms, and follow-up schedules.
- Prepare and submit a Final Consultancy Report documenting the overall implementation of the assignment, including methodology, activities conducted, outputs delivered, key accomplishments, lessons learned, implementation challenges, recommendations, and sustainability measures to support the continued integration of MHPSS into MNCH services beyond the consultancy period.
Activities shall be completed with all required deliverable reports submitted by email to IOM no later than 16 March 2027 (40% of overall contract value), upon satisfactory completion and acceptance of the deliverable.
