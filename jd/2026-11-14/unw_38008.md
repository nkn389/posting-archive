Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security.

Summary of Country Profile

The armed conflict in Sudan, now in its fourth year, has resulted in the world’s largest humanitarian crisis, with an estimated 33.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 8.6 million internally displaced across all 18 states, amid acute food insecurity, near-collapse of basic services and repeated attacks on civilians, particularly in Darfur and Kordofan. Women and girls bear a disproportionate burden and impact of the conflict. Sexual violence has been used systematically as a weapon of war, and the number of women and girls needing gender-based violence (GBV) services has quadrupled since the conflict began.

Women-led organizations (WLOs) are at the forefront of the humanitarian response and local peacebuilding, often reaching communities that others actors cannot access. In 2025, 85 WLOs surveyed by UN Women reported supporting more than 17.5 million people, underscoring their critical role in delivering assistance, strengthening community resilience and advancing locally led peacebuilding. Yet, despite their contribution, WLOs continue to face significant funding and capacity constraints, with limited access to humanitarian financing and continued exclusion from formal peace processes.

Against this backdrop, aligned to the UN Women Strategic Plan, relevant regional and country planning frameworks, and the Sudan UN Interim Cooperation Framework, the UN Women Sudan Country Office implements programmes across the country. Guided by the UN Women Sudan Strategic Note 2026–2028, the country office prioritizes women’s leadership and meaningful participation in peace processes and mediation; gender in humanitarian action (GiHA) and coordination; prevention of and response to GBV and conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV); women’s economic empowerment and resilient livelihoods for displaced and host-community women; and strengthening and resourcing women led organizations, including through the Women, Peace and Humanitarian Fund (WPHF).UN Women Sudan plays a strategic role in advancing the Women, Peace and Security (WPS) agenda, strengthening gender-responsive humanitarian action, and ensuring that women's leadership, priorities and rights are integrated into peacebuilding, recovery and development processes. The Country Office works through national institutions, women-led organizations, civil society networks and UN system coordination mechanisms to strengthen local leadership, accountability and sustainable results in an exceptionally complex operating environment.

Under the overall supervision of UN Women Regional Director for East and Southern Africa, the Country Representative is responsible for providing strategic direction to advancing gender equality and women's empowerment across the humanitarian-development-peace nexus in Sudan. The Country Representative positions UN Women strategically within the UNCT, HCT and broader international engagement on Sudan, while strengthening partnerships with national and local actors, regional institutions and international partners.

As a UNCT member, the Country Representative has mutual accountability with the Resident Coordinator for periodically reporting on contributions to, and support for the implementation of the United Nations Interim Cooperation Framework (UNICF), including substantive contribution to the Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) and the UNCT-SWAP Gender Equality Scorecard, among other planning and gender accountability frameworks.

The Country Representative is responsible for leading and managing the Sudan Country Office (CO) portfolio and for translating the UN Women's Strategic Plan (SP) into humanitarian, peacebuilding and development initiatives and results by developing, implementing and managing programmes and projects under the Sudan CO; and developing effective partnerships with Government counterparts, UN system agencies and organizations, civil society and women led organizations, and with bilateral and non-traditional donors in order to promote inter-agency partnerships, support for UN reform, and mobilization of visibility and resources to advance gender equality and women's empowerment.
