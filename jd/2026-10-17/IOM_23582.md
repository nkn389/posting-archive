The International Organization for Migration (IOM), in cooperation with Asian Development Bank (ADB) and the governments of Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, is implementing the project “Cross-border Health Security Partnership between the Republic of Tajikistan and the Republic of Uzbekistan”, launched in September 2026 under the ADB Technical Assistance entitled: Addressing Health Threats in Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation Countries and the Caucasus. The project responds to persistent public health risks, including insufficient coordination between countries, gaps in disease surveillance and data exchange, and limited understanding of the health vulnerabilities of migrants and mobile populations. The proposed project foresees activities along the economic corridor Khujand-Tashkent, more specifically in the area of the Points of Entry "Fotehobod", district Mastchoh (Tajikistan) and "Oybek", district Bekabad (Uzbekistan).
Under the project, the health needs assessment of vulnerable groups and mobile populations (including migrant workers) in border communities near these Points of Entry is envisioned. Topics to cover are health profiles (including self-reported morbidity in particular chronic diseases), socioeconomic status including mobility, barriers to receiving health services, health needs, the impact of infectious disease (chronic and/or outbreaks) on livelihoods and human mobility dimensions of communicable disease, and sex-differentiated effects of chronic infectious disease and/or outbreaks. The findings will help identify priority areas for intervention and inform practical measures to strengthen mobility-sensitive and migrant-inclusive primary health care services, improve community-based communicable disease prevention and response, and enhance access to appropriate health services for migrants, mobile populations, and host communities.

Project Context and Scope
The overall objective of this assignment is to design and lead a mixed-method (qualitative and quantitative) health needs assessment of vulnerable groups and mobile populations, including migrant workers, in selected border communities of Tajikistan and Uzbekistan in order to generate evidence on their health needs, vulnerabilities, health-related risks, barriers to accessing health services, and priority concerns.

Under the overall supervision of IOM Uzbekistan and IOM Tajikistan and in coordination with the national consultants, the International Consultant will perform the following tasks:
1. Develop a work plan in coordination with IOM Project Manager and Bilateral Task Force Group (BTFG) outlining the geographical scope, data collection methodology, key activities, timeline and deliverables.
2. Develop the mixed-method assessment methodology and data collection tools (questionnaires, interview guides) based on WHO assessment tools, including the assessment scope and indicators, sampling approach, data collection framework, data quality assurance procedures, analytical methodology, in cooperation with national consultants and in consultation with IOM and relevant stakeholders.
3. Coordinate and monitor the National Consultant through online consultations during field implementation, including briefing on the assessment methodology; oversee the consistent application of the methodology and tools; monitor data quality and completeness; and address methodological and technical issues arising during data collection.
4. Incorporate the findings of the stock-taking and desk review of border health and cross-border assessments conducted by National Consultant and the mapping and identification of key border communities and key vulnerability groups along the mobility corridor between “Fotehobod” and “Oybek” to the final assessment report.
5. Review collected data and preliminary findings to ensure data completeness, consistency, accuracy and methodological rigor, identifying and addressing data gaps or inconsistencies in coordination with the national consultants.
6. Prepare a comprehensive needs assessment report in accordance with the IOM House Style and relevant IOM editorial, branding, and reporting standards, presenting key findings, identified gaps and bottlenecks, and practical, prioritized recommendations.
7. Review and provide recommendations to the joint strategy – developed by the National Consultant - on reducing health risks among the most vulnerable populations in border areas (women, older persons, children, persons with disability, and migrant and mobile populations) from chronic diseases and/or infectious disease outbreaks.
8. Prepare and deliver presentations of the preliminary and final assessment findings to IOM, government counterparts and relevant stakeholders, facilitate discussion of key findings and recommendations, and incorporate relevant feedback into the final assessment report.
9. Consolidate survey methodology, processes, tools, results, recommendations for strategy development and lessons learned into a package for potential use by other CAREC countries.

- Third deliverable: A comprehensive draft needs assessment report submitted to IOM, including methodology, findings, gap analysis, conclusions, and recommendations. First instalment payment - 15 days, deliverable by 25 December 2026.
