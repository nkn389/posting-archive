The International Organization for Migration (IOM), in cooperation with Asian Development Bank (ADB) and the governments of Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, is implementing the project “Cross-border Health Security Partnership between the Republic of Tajikistan and the Republic of Uzbekistan”, launched in September 2026 under the ADB Technical Assistance entitled: Addressing Health Threats in Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation Countries and the Caucasus. The project responds to persistent public health risks, including insufficient coordination between countries, gaps in disease surveillance and data exchange, and limited understanding of the health vulnerabilities of migrants and mobile populations. The proposed project foresees activities along the economic corridor Khujand-Tashkent, more specifically in the area of the Points of Entry (PoE) "Fotehobod", district Mastchoh (Tajikistan) and "Oybek", district Bekabad (Uzbekistan).

Under the project, the assessment on health service availability and readiness in selected border regions and along mobility corridors surrounding the "Fotehobod" in Tajikistan and "Oybek" PoE in Uzbekistan is envisioned. The findings of the health service availability assessment will be used to develop responses in relation to community health risk reduction and improved service availability in selected border regions. This evidence generated will further contribute to cross-border cooperation and communication between Tajikistan and Uzbekistan by identifying critical issues and gaps which can guide the priorities of the Project Bilateral Task Force Group to: (a) develop surveillance and control measures for health security, including timely recognition, reporting, referral and management of suspected cases; and (b) establishing cross-border communication and coordination protocols or mechanisms for providing timely and urgent cross-border data sharing on the local level (through bilateral committees etc.). Based on this assessment, Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) on cross-border information and data exchange on infectious and endemic diseases (between countries and/or between PoE) will be developed and tested. SOPs will include topics such as case management and referrals, disease surveillance, infection prevention and control and coordination on local levels during outbreaks and/or public health events, by safeguarding confidentiality of patient information.
Project Context and Scope
The overall objective of this assignment is to support the Government of Tajikistan and the Government of Uzbekistan in generating evidence on the capacity of health systems in border areas to respond to the needs of migrants, mobile populations, and host communities, particularly in the context of communicable disease prevention, detection, referral, and response.

Under the direct supervision of the IOM Project Manager and the technical guidance of the International Consultant, and in coordination with the Bilateral Task Force Group and relevant national and local stakeholders, the National Consultant will undertake the following tasks:
1. Review and support the contextualization of the assessment methodology, tools and data collection instruments developed by the International Consultant, in consultation with IOM and relevant stakeholders, including health facility and PoE assessment questionnaires, observation checklists, key informant interview guides, referral pathway mapping tools, and other relevant instruments.
2. Conduct mapping of existing cross-border data sharing systems, policies, and/or agreements on providing health services and/or diagnostics (if any) to cross-border migrant workers and mobile populations to lay the foundation for an electronic medical record system (including for chronic infectious diseases) and financial protection mechanism for migrants and mobile populations crossing borders to access medical services and diagnostics.
3. Conduct a desk review of relevant legislation, policies, protocols, bilateral agreements, surveillance systems, and existing SOPs related to border health and cross-border disease control.
4. In consultation with the BTFG, consolidate a list of priority infectious and endemic diseases of cross-border relevance to guide the protocols on risk assessment, timeliness and scope of information to share between the two countries.
5. Support the BTFG in planning and organization of field activities, including identification and coordination with selected health facilities, Points of Entry (PoEs), relevant authorities and other stakeholders in the selected border areas and along mobility corridors.
6. Conduct a field assessment with BTFG using the agreed methodology and tools, including KoboToolbox, and conduct interviews and consultations with relevant health, border and other stakeholders.
7. Clean, organize and validate quantitative and qualitative data collected through the assessment, identify data gaps or inconsistencies, and provide the validated data to the International Consultant for further analysis.
8. Prepare a field assessment report summarizing field activities, key observations, preliminary findings, challenges and country-specific issues identified during the assessment and provide inputs to the International Consultant for preparation of the overall assessment report.
9. Develop an operational roadmap on improving health service availability and readiness for vulnerable groups, migrants and mobile populations in border communities with multisectoral recommendations and of relevance to cross-border coordination in coordination with the BTFG.
10. Develop SOPs covering, at minimum: cross-border disease notification and reporting; information and data exchange mechanisms; case management and referral procedures; disease surveillance and outbreak response; infection prevention and control measures; roles and responsibilities of participating institutions; confidentiality and data protection requirements; and communication and coordination during public health emergencies.
11. Facilitate stakeholder consultations and validation workshops on the draft SOPs and facilitate a tabletop and/or simulation exercise.
Performance indicators for the evaluation of results

- First deliverable: Completed field assessment report, summarizing field activities, key observations, preliminary findings, challenges and country-specific issues identified during the assessment and provide inputs to the International Consultant for preparation of the overall assessment report. 8 days, deliverable by 20 November 2026.
- Second deliverable: Draft health service availability and readiness assessment report, including analysis of findings, identified gaps and bottlenecks, preliminary recommendations and priority actions, prepared in coordination with the International Consultant; developed draft SOPs; Report on mapping of existing cross-border data sharing systems; list of priority infectious and endemic diseases of cross-border relevance - First instalment payment - 10 days, deliverable by 25 December 2026.
- Third deliverable: Finalized health service availability and readiness assessment report, incorporating feedback from IOM and the Bilateral Task Force Group and including final recommendations and prioritized actions; finalized SOPs incorporating feedback from the BTFG and IOM; facilitated validation workshop/s and tabletop and/or simulation exercise. - Second and final instalment payment – 7 days, deliverable by 15 March 2027.
