Overview

The World Vegetable Center (WorldVeg) is a non-profit, autonomous international agricultural research center with headquarters in Taiwan and five regional offices around the globe. WorldVeg conducts research and development programs that contribute to realizing the potential of vegetables for healthier lives and more resilient livelihoods.
WorldVeg holds the world’s largest public collection of vegetable genetic resources. The Center’s vegetable breeding programs in Africa and Asia turn these resources into improved breeding lines and work with the public and private sectors to develop and scale new vegetable varieties for the benefit of smallholder farmers and consumers in lower-income countries. The Center also develops and promotes safer and more sustainable production and postharvest practices to make vegetable value chains more efficient with the ultimate aim of making vegetables more available, affordable, and accessible. For more information, please visit our website: worldveg.org
About the HERS Program
The WorldVeg Center intends to implement a program known as Her Entrepreneurial Road to Success (HERS). HERS is a four-year rural economic transformation program designed to support and facilitate the transition of young rural women aged 18–35 from low-return, fragmented income-generating activities to viable, market-oriented enterprises within horticultural value chains.
The model integrates three mutually reinforcing pillars: (i) community governance and social transformation; (ii) enhancement of horticultural value chains and market linkages; and (iii) business development and access to finance. The program combines community-level enabling conditions, practical horticultural capacity building and enterprise development, market integration, and sequenced access to finance.
Working in partnership with Cross Boundary and Tostan as Implementing Partners (IP), the program will cover 730 communities in Senegal and The Gambia. This is the first WorldVeg grant from this Partner. WorldVeg will lead and coordinate the partnership and will bear full accountability for program integration, implementation excellence, and reporting. The implementation of HERS requires strong project management, coordination, oversight, and cross-team collaboration.
As the Primary Grantee (PG), WorldVeg is fully accountable for the implementation of the HERS program in accordance with the contract signed between WorldVeg and the partner. The primary guarantee serves as the formal link to the partner’s Executive Committee, overseeing overall results delivery, coordination of the Program Steering Committee, financial management, environmental and social safeguards, gender inclusion, and outcome monitoring. In addition to its role as the PG, WorldVeg leads the program’s horticulture component (Pillar 2). This dual role as PG and IP means WorldVeg must oversee the implementation of the entire program and coordinate the work of the Implementing Partners, while also delivering its own area of work, Pillar 2 (horticultural value chains).

Key Responsibilities

Position overview
The Program Director holds overall accountability for the HERS Program, ensuring it delivers the results committed to the partner, within the approved budget and in line with agreed policies and procedures. The SPC safeguards WorldVeg’s institutional accountability as the Primary Grantee ensuring quality results as per agreed workplan and budget as well as compliance with the partner’s fiduciary, safeguard, and reporting obligations.
The PD will also oversee the contractual partnership arrangements with the Implementing Partners and provide direction to the Pillar Leads in Senegal and The Gambia to ensure program alignment, coordination, stakeholder engagement, technical quality and results delivery, compliance, and reporting obligations. The PD will be the principal focal point in WorldVeg’s relationship with the partner specifically in relation to matters pertaining to the HERS Program. Within WorldVeg, the PD will report to the Regional Director, West and Central Africa office. They will also engage with WorldVeg’s Technical Program Directorates and Action Area Leads, as relevant on plans and budgets relating to the WorldVeg component (Pillar 2). In collaboration with WorldVeg’s Impact Evaluation Team, the Program Director will ensure that the results, lessons, and impacts are robustly monitored, documented, and shared as required.
