Organizational Context and Scope
Established in 1951, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) is a Related Organization of the United Nations, and as the leading UN agency in the field of migration, works closely with governmental, intergovernmental and non-governmental partners. IOM is dedicated to promoting humane and orderly migration for the benefit of all.
According to the Notre Dame Global Adaptation Initiative (ND-GAIN) index, Madagascar is among the least prepared countries to deal with climate shocks, ranking 174th out of 181 countries. Deforestation is critical: it is estimated that 30% of forests have been lost since 2001, and the province of Toamasina alone accounts for 55% of national forest losses. The municipality of Andasibe presents a strategic paradox: as a global biodiversity centre home to the endemic Indri Indri and the Andasibe-Mantadia National Park, Madagascar's leading ecotourism destination, it is facing accelerated deforestation linked to a strong dependence on wood energy. This dynamic is compounded by the pronounced landlocked nature of many fokontany, some of which are hamlets, located up to 12 km from the communal capital, are only accessible on foot. Deprived of regular access to markets, basic services and employment opportunities, rural households are turning to charcoal and firewood as immediately accessible sources of income; internal migration flows to the park area are increasing this pressure.
In response to this challenge, IOM is implementing a pilot programme titled “Integrated Territorial Model of Andasibe: Clean Cooking, Reforestation, and Rural Roads” articulating the transition to clean cooking, ecological restoration and rural access planning. Aligned with Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 7 and 13, the National Clean Cooking Development Policy, the Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) and the National Strategy and Action Plan for Biodiversity (NBSAP), the project aims to constitute a model that can be replicated at the national level.
Under the direct supervision of the Chief of Mission (COM) in Madagascar and, in collaboration with relevant units at IOM Madagascar, the National Project Development and Reporting Officer will be responsible and accountable for supporting the strategic development, coordination and expansion of IOM Madagascar’s programme portfolio through project development, donor reporting, resource mobilization and partnership engagement. The successful candidate will contribute to the identification of emerging migration, climate resilience, environmental sustainability, displacement, protection and development priorities in Madagascar and support the formulation of results-based programmes and projects aligned with IOM’s Strategic Results Framework, regional priorities, national development objectives and donor requirements. Particular emphasis will be placed on supporting the implementation, reporting, visibility and resource mobilization efforts of the “Integrated Territorial Model of Andasibe: Clean Cooking, Reforestation, and Rural Roads” project. The National Project Development and Reporting Officer will support donor reporting, results monitoring, partnership engagement and the identification of opportunities to scale up and replicate the integrated territorial approach in Madagascar.
