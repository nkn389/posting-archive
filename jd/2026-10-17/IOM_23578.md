BACKGROUND
The International Organization for Migration (IOM), in cooperation with Asian Development Bank (ADB) and the governments of Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, is implementing the project “Cross-border Health Security Partnership between the Republic of Tajikistan and the Republic of Uzbekistan”, launched in September 2026 under the ADB Technical Assistance entitled: Addressing Health Threats in Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation Countries and the Caucasus. The project responds to persistent public health risks, including insufficient coordination between countries, gaps in disease surveillance and data exchange, and limited understanding of the health vulnerabilities of migrants and mobile populations. The proposed project foresees activities along the economic corridor Khujand-Tashkent, more specifically in the area of the Points of Entry (PoE) "Fotehobod", district Mastchoh (Tajikistan) and "Oybek", district Bekabad (Uzbekistan).

Under the project, the assessment on health service availability and readiness in selected border regions and along mobility corridors surrounding the "Fotehobod" and "Oybek" points of entry is envisioned. The findings of the health service availability assessment will be used to develop responses in relation to community health risk reduction and improved service availability in selected border regions. This evidence generated will further contribute to cross-border cooperation and communication between Tajikistan and Uzbekistan by identifying critical issues and gaps which can guide the priorities of the Project Bilateral Task Force Group (BTFG) to: (a) develop surveillance and control measures for health security, including timely recognition, reporting, referral and management of suspected cases; and (b) establishing cross-border communication and coordination protocols or mechanisms for providing timely and urgent cross-border data sharing on the local level (through bilateral committees etc.)
Project Context and Scope
The overall objective of this assignment is to support the Government of Tajikistan and the Government of Uzbekistan in generating evidence on the capacity of health systems in border areas to respond to the needs of migrants, mobile populations, and host communities, particularly in the context of communicable disease prevention, detection, referral, and response. The International Consultant will design and lead a mixed-method (qualitative and quantitative) assessment of health availability and readiness in selected border regions /along mobility corridors around the Points of Entry "Fotehobod" and "Oybek" in cooperation with the BTFG and other relevant stakeholders.

Under the overall supervision of IOM Uzbekistan and IOM Tajikistan and in coordination with the national consultants, the Consultant will perform the following tasks:
1. Develop a work plan in coordination with IOM Project Manager outlining the data collection methodology summary, key activities, timeline, and deliverables.
2. Develop the mixed-method assessment methodology and data collection tools based on WHO assessment tools, including the assessment scope and indicators, sampling and facility selection approach, data collection framework, data quality assurance procedures, analytical methodology, structured questionnaires for health facilities and PoE, observation checklists, key informant interview guides, and referral pathway mapping tools, in cooperation with national consultants and in consultation with IOM and relevant stakeholders.
3. Coordinate and monitor the lead National Consultant – who in turn is overseeing the data collection conducted by the BTFG - during field implementation, including briefing on the assessment methodology; oversee the consistent application of the methodology and tools; monitor data quality and completeness; and address methodological and technical issues arising during data collection.
4. Review and validate collected data and review preliminary findings to ensure data completeness, consistency, accuracy and methodological rigor, identifying and addressing data gaps or inconsistencies in coordination with the national consultants.
5. Prepare a comprehensive health service availability and readiness assessment report in accordance with IOM House Style and relevant IOM editorial, branding, and reporting standards, presenting key findings, incorporated the findings of the mapping of existing cross-border data sharing systems and/or agreements on providing health services or diagnostics conducted by the national consultant, identified gaps and bottlenecks, and practical, prioritized recommendations and actions for strengthening health service readiness and referral mechanisms in the selected border areas and along mobility corridors.
6. Prepare and deliver presentations of the preliminary and final assessment findings to IOM, government counterparts and relevant stakeholders, facilitate discussion of key findings and recommendations, and incorporate relevant feedback into the final assessment report.
7. Review and provide written technical recommendations to the development of a joint strategy on reducing health risks among the most vulnerable populations in border areas (women, older persons, children, persons with disability, and migrant and mobile populations) from chronic diseases and/or infectious disease outbreaks.
8. Review and provide written technical recommendations for the development of an operational roadmap on improving health service availability and readiness for vulnerable groups, migrants and mobile populations in border communities with multisectoral recommendations and of relevance to cross-border coordination.
9. Consolidate survey methodology, processes, tools, results, recommendations for strategy development and lessons learned into a package for potential use by other CAREC countries.
