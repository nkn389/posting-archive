Project Context and Scope
The International Organization for Migration (IOM) works to strengthen the protection environment for displaced populations, migrants, returnees, and vulnerable communities in Pakistan. Within the context of increased movements of Afghan nationals between Pakistan and Afghanistan, evolving protection risks, return dynamics, legal and documentation challenges, and heightened vulnerabilities among different population groups, IOM seeks to commission a comprehensive qualitative and participatory research study.

The study will replicate and adapt the methodology used in the regional "Affective Cartographies" study while expanding its focus beyond women and girls to include:

• Adolescents
• Adult women
• Adult men
• Older persons
• Persons with disabilities
• Female-headed households
• Survivors of violence
• Persons at risk of trafficking, exploitation, detention, or deportation
• Valid VISA holders, documented (former) and undocumented Afghan nationals

The research will examine the experiences of Afghan nationals and other migrants with protection concerns through protection lens exploring:
1. Individual Sphere
2. Family and Community Sphere
3. Institutional Sphere
4. Cross-Border Sphere

The consultancy will generate evidence to support protection programming, policy engagement, advocacy, protection mainstreaming, community-based protection initiatives, legal assistance interventions, MHPSS programming, durable solutions planning, and future strategic investments by IOM and partners.

Organizational Department / Unit to which the Consultant is contributing

The consultant will contribute to the Protection Unit, Humanitarian, Recovery and Resilience Division (HRRD), IOM Pakistan. The Protection Unit implements activities related to protection case management, gender-based violence, child protection, legal assistance, community engagement, protection mainstreaming, and evidence-based protection analysis through protection monitoring.

To design and implement a comprehensive protection research study that maps:

• Protection barriers
