Project Context and Scope
IOM’s Regional Data Hub (RDH) provides data and analysis expertise and guidance for implementing the IOM Strategic Plan. The RDH strengthens the regional evidence base on migration, including on migrants in vulnerable situations, by providing technical support and capacity development to IOM offices, regional bodies, member states and other stakeholders on migration and displacement data; and supporting the uptake of knowledge and evidence to inform policies and programmes. Activities and objectives of the RDH are in line with the priorities of the IOM Migration Data Strategy to: 1) strengthen the regional evidence base on migration; 2) develop the data and information management capacities of states, Regional Economic Communities and other key partners; and 3) ensure more evidence-based IOM and UN-wide engagement in the region.

Given the risks of human trafficking, and the widespread use of smuggling on the migration routes in the region, IOM also implements a project entitled “Regional Counter Smuggling Programme” (RCSP) funded by the United Kingdom’s Home Office. The RCSP is a three-year regional initiative that aims to strengthen border management capacities at airports and enhance regional cooperation to tackle document fraud, migrant smuggling, and related transnational organised crime (TOC) in the East and Horn of Africa. The programme addresses critical technical, operational and institutional gaps at major international airports while promoting protection-sensitive approaches that safeguard migrant rights and dignity. By tackling Organised Immigration Crime (OIC) linked to migrant smuggling and document fraud that facilitate irregular migration and expose migrants to risks of misinformation, abuse, exploitation, and, in some cases, trafficking in persons (TiP), this programme addresses significant migration-related challenges while reinforcing national security.

The consultant will be hired to deliver a report on an instalment payment basis, alongside a regional consultant and IOM staff and stakeholders:
Equip government stakeholder to improve their country’s data management capacity to generate deidentified, administrative data and statistics on irregular migration, trafficking in persons and smuggling of migrants particularly at air borders.
1. First installment upon delivery of the completion of an assessment and a report that captures:

- Review recent assessments of existing government data management processes for their ability to generate accurate, reliable, consistent and trusted migration, trafficking, and smuggling statistics.

- Fill any remaining gaps through primary data collection that need to be filled in order to have an exhaustive and comprehensive understanding of current data processes in Kenya.

- In coordination with IOM and government stakeholders, draft country-level data-related processes for national TIP, smuggling of migrants (SOM) and irregular migration administrative data in line with the new International Classification for Administrative Data on Trafficking in Persons, most effectively IC-TIP.

- Obtain review and validation of the data and proposed data processes by Government officials of Kenya.

2. The second installment is paid upon delivery of:

- Adapt existing data collection tools and TiP materials for each national context and integrate new content on SoM and irregular migration into existing data collection tools. Tools will be reviewed and validated by Government of Kenya stakeholders.

- Create bespoke capacity development materials per country that can sustainably equip Government of Kenya agencies to establish or improve their collection, classification, and report administrative data on trafficking in persons in pursuit of more consistent, comparable, and actionable trafficking data.

- Lead/deliver capacity development programs including at least 1 workshop and 1 training per country of interest, with additional training defined by stakeholders in advance, in coordination with IOM Kenya office, a regional consultant and relevant stakeholders.

3. Third is paid upon delivery of:

- Evaluation report of capacity building measures for their adoption success;

- Pathway report that provides guidance on how to integrate administrative data into national statistical frameworks per country; how to leverage administrative data for national statistics and how the data can be used to develop the evidence base for policy and programmatic responses, reviewed and validated by government stakeholders.

- Develop means to monitor the sustained success of the linkage such that administrative data is collected and used for counter-TIP and counter-SoM. This may include selecting the priority sources of administrative data, drafting reporting frameworks, and recommended actions, developing inter-agency collaboration frameworks or beyond.

Depending on the expertise of the consultant, a fourth deliverable will include the following activities to strengthen relevant National Referral Mechanisms through the provision of data from government data systems. If the identified consultant has proven capacity and evidenced expertise in the protection of migrants, case management or similar, a fourth deliverable will be paid upon the delivery of the following activities:

- Assess readiness of institutions and referral mechanisms such as the NRM.

- Support government to align their data systems with international trafficking data standards.

- Support governments to standardize trafficking-related data collection.

- Develop data collection tools to inform the NRM update and Minimum Service Standards document development.

- In coordination with Government partners, review and update the existing National Referral Mechanism to align with border management processes for the purpose of referring travellers appropriately, as relevant

- Develop a comprehensive information management flow document that will be annexed to the SOP of the country’s National Referral Mechanism.

- Obtain external validation of the revised NRM and SOP and disseminate.
