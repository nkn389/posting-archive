&lt;h3&gt;&lt;span class="emphasis-2"&gt;&lt;b&gt;&lt;u&gt;What We Can Achieve Together:&lt;/u&gt;&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;br /&gt;&lt;p&gt;As the Tucson Campus Maintenance Manager with The Nature Conservancy (TNC), you’ll help keep the places that support Arizona’s conservation work safe, functional, and environmentally sustainable. Working with the Arizona team and reporting to the Engagement &amp; Operations Director, you’ll care for the Tucson campus’s buildings, rental facilities, grounds, fleet vehicles, tools, equipment, and mechanical systems, while also providing facilities support to Arizona preserves and other leased spaces. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;br /&gt;&lt;p&gt;Your work will combine hands-on maintenance with planning and coordination. You’ll organize preventive maintenance and repairs, coordinate contractors and vendors, oversee safety inspections, and help prioritize facilities improvements. You’ll support purchasing and budgets, maintain records and procedures, and coordinate project timelines to keep work on schedule and within budget. Environmental sustainability will be part of your everyday work, including support for water conservation, recycling, and composting systems. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;br /&gt;&lt;p&gt;You’ll collaborate with campus colleagues and preserve staff to identify maintenance needs and practical solutions that support safe, sustainable operations. The role includes regular physical exertion; regularly lifting, carrying, pushing, and pulling 35–50 pounds; climbing tall ladders; and working underground with the water cistern system. Work may involve infrequent exposure to disagreeable elements, occasional travel with overnight stays within Arizona and elsewhere in the United States, and occasional long hours, weekends, and holidays. TNC youth safety training is completed within the first 30 days of employment and annually thereafter. &lt;/p&gt;&lt;br /&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;span class="emphasis-2"&gt;&lt;b&gt;&lt;u&gt;We’re Looking for You:&lt;/u&gt;&lt;/b&gt;&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;br /&gt;&lt;p&gt;What if your commitment to caring for buildings and landscapes could help sustain the places where Arizona conservation happens?
