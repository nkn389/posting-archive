Project Context and Scope
In 2026, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) in Belarus launched the project "Strengthening the National Capacity of the Republic of Belarus to Identify Victims of Human Trafficking within Migration Flows, Including Foreign Nationals."

The project aims to strengthen the response to human trafficking in the Republic of Belarus by enhancing the effectiveness of the identification, referral, and protection of victims of human trafficking within migration flows, including foreign nationals. The initiative focuses on building the institutional capacity of key stakeholders, including the Ministry of Internal Affairs, the State Border Committee, the Ministry of Labor and Social Protection, and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, as well as regional Multidisciplinary Groups (MDGs) responsible for the identification, referral, and reintegration of human trafficking victims.

The project includes a comprehensive desk review/mapping to take stock of existing national legislative and operational frameworks and current practices related to the identification of victims of trafficking within migration flows, including the mapping of existing procedures and mechanisms for interagency cooperation. The desk review will identify gaps and areas requiring further improvement.

In order to provide expert and advisory support for the development of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and/or an interagency algorithm, the project includes six meetings of MDGs tasked with the identification, referral, and reintegration of victims of human trafficking across the country.

Based on the findings and recommendations of the MDGs, interagency SOPs and/or an interaction algorithm for the identification and referral of human trafficking victims within migration flows, including foreign nationals, will be developed, endorsed, and disseminated among all relevant stakeholders.

Considering the above, IOM Belarus is announcing a vacancy for a consultant to deliver these activities between early November 2026 and late March 2027.

Tasks to be performed under this contract

- A comprehensive desk review/mapping to take stock of existing national legislative and operational frameworks and current practices in Belarus related to the identification of victims of trafficking within migration flows has been conducted and includes the mapping of existing procedures, coordination and cooperation mechanisms among relevant stakeholders, and a review of practices against applicable international standards and good practices.
- A desk review report is available, assessing the effectiveness of existing identification and referral mechanisms, identifying key gaps and needs, and providing recommendations for strengthening the national system for the identification and referral of victims of human trafficking within migration flows.
- Expert support has been provided to six Multidisciplinary Working Groups meetings across the regions of Belarus. Proposals, recommendations, and identified needs from participating stakeholders have been collected and analysed and subsequently consolidated into a dedicated report to inform the development of the SOPs/ interagency algorithm.
- Based on the findings of the analysis, inputs from the MDGs, stakeholder consultations, and international best practices, draft Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)/an interagency algorithm for the identification and referral of victims of human trafficking within migration flows, including foreign nationals, have been developed.
- The draft SOPs/interagency algorithm have been submitted to the project’s Steering Committee and relevant stakeholders for review and approval. Feedback and recommendations received during the review process have been systematically analysed and incorporated into the revised draft.
- The final version of the SOPs/interagency algorithm for the identification and referral of victims of human trafficking within migration flows, including foreign nationals, has been prepared and submitted to the project’s Steering Committee for final review and approval.
- Following completion and endorsement, the SOPs/interagency algorithm have been formally handed over to IOM Belarus, the Ministry of Internal Affairs of the Republic of Belarus, and other relevant stakeholders to support their implementation, dissemination, and future application.
