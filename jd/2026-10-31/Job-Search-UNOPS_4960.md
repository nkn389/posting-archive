Job Highlight

This is a unique opportunity to originate and implement innovative and sustainable financing solutions within a global portfolio. The role allows you to shape new financial instruments, influence policy, and drive transformative change across programmatic areas such as health, climate, energy, environment, food, nutrition, and water and sanitation. It offers the chance to blend financial expertise with development goals, engage high-level stakeholders, and contribute to sustainable impact through entrepreneurial design and strategic partnerships.

About the Group

The Infrastructure and Project Management Group (IPMG) provides policy, standards guidance and support in three UNOPS service lines of Project Management, Infrastructure and Financial Management Services (Grant Support and Fund Management). IPMG leads the design and implementation of a project, programme and portfolio management (P3M) system centered around the needs of UNOPS partners, beneficiaries and frontline personnel. IPMG also drives UNOPS efforts to leverage sustainable, resilient and inclusive infrastructure and maximize local capacity through grant support management to achieve sustainable development. Through thought leadership, we aim to position UNOPS with its partners, foster innovative solutions for sustainable and inclusive development, and accelerate the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). This includes speeding up the implementation of urgent infrastructure and scaling up infrastructure service delivery, particularly in relation to climate action and energy. Our delivery approach to project management, infrastructure and grant support management prioritizes development needs, empowers institutions and communities, builds partners’ capacity, champions gender equality and social inclusion and reduces environmental impact. IPMG translates policy into practice by providing strategic direction, standards and oversight, while facilitating knowledge sharing and capacity-building for UNOPS teams globally.

Job Specific Context

Under the direct supervision of the Geneva Head of Programme, this role provides expert advisory services to UNOPS Projects, Programmes and hosted entities, developing innovative and sustainable financing mechanisms and return on investment (ROI) instruments primarily for Geneva and WEC portfolio. This role will also be part of UNOPS corporate retainer pool, may support UNOPS offices in other regions, depending on business needs. This role involves navigating the legal, operational, and financial complexities within the UNOPS framework and donor expectations.

Role Purpose

The Innovative and Sustainable Finance Advisor will conduct research, engage relevant stakeholders, assess the feasibility of various financing mechanisms, and provide practical recommendations for establishing a legally, operationally, and financially viable model(s) that aligns with UNOPS mission and donor expectations.

Functions / Key Results Expected

On an as-needed/on-call basis, initiated through formal Task Orders, the Innovative and Sustainable Finance Advisor will be responsible for the following functions:

1. Research and Landscape Analysis:

- Conduct a comprehensive review of innovative and sustainable financing mechanisms, such as but not limited to: venture philanthropy models, accelerator programmes, venture funds, catalytic financing approaches, and blended finance mechanisms relevant to supported programmes
- Review comparable financing models used by multilateral organizations, development finance institutions, philanthropic organizations, and private sector investors.
- Assess lessons learned and best practices applicable to UNOPS projects, programmes, funds and hosted entities (including Geneva).

2. Stakeholder Consultation and Coordination:

- Consult with relevant UNOPS departments, including the Legal Group, Infrastructure and Project Management Group (IPMG), Finance Group (FG), Procurement Group, and Partnerships and Futures Group, to assess institutional feasibility.
- Consult selected external experts in innovative and sustainable financing, including but not limited to accelerator, venture and impact investing, development finance, innovation, and global development, as appropriate.
- Facilitate discussions to validate findings and recommendations across key internal and external stakeholders.
- Consult the various hosted entities and funds to gauge their interest in potential collaboration or joint investments, when appropriate for joint programming.

3. Framework Integration:

- Provide strategic guidance on embedding innovative and/or sustainable financing mechanisms into UNOPS corporate policies, operational strategies, and risk management frameworks.
- Thematic Scaling: Identify high-impact opportunities to scale financial models across core sectors, including climate action, global health, education, and humanitarian response.
- Institutional Impact: Assess the legal, fiduciary, governance, financial, and operational implications of introducing new financing mechanisms across UNOPS corporate structures, hosted entities, managed funds, and project and programme governance bodies.
- Risk Management: Identify sector-specific risks and mitigation measures.
- Mechanism Analysis: Define and evaluate Return on Investment (ROI) and impact-linked instruments, including green/blue bonds, recoverable grants, revenue-sharing arrangements, royalties, milestone-based repayments, and revolving funds.
- Comparative Assessment: Analyze the comparative advantages, limitations, risk profiles, and operational requirements of each financing mechanism.

4. Operational Implementation:
Develop comprehensive innovative and/or sustainable finance frameworks and policy recommendations, incorporating technical parameters.

- Corporate Boundaries: Define policy limits and rules regarding holding, receiving, and recycling financial assets (e.g., equity, bonds, royalties, revolving facilities). Support the preparation of project/programme policy exception requests as needed. Recommend policy strengthening language to IPMG and FG.
- Instrument Mapping: Identify and align financial tools with entity types and product/service maturity stages (e.g., health, technology, infrastructure, service delivery) with the project or programme impact goals.
- Jurisdictional Compliance: Evaluate cross-border regulatory constraints, including international sanctions, tax structures, withholding requirements, and legal compliance.
- Risk Management: Establish oversight requirements, and design concrete risk-mitigation measures.
- Impact monitoring: Define impact measurement methodologies.
- Term-Sheet Architecture: Draft initial operational frameworks and term-sheet outlines for prioritized financial instruments.
- Personnel Support: Advise and train UNOPS personnel on designing, structuring, and managing robust financial architectures, including blended finance tools, green bonds, and impact metrics.

5. Reports and Presentation:

- Develop executive summaries, presentations, and briefing materials summarizing key findings and recommendations.
- Present findings to leadership, teams, and external partners as requested.

6. Other Duties :

- Ensure gender and social inclusion considerations, and the adoption of sustainable and environmentally responsible practices, are integrated into all aspects of project design and implementation.
