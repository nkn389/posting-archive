Introduction
Established in 1951, IOM is a Related Organization of the United Nations and the leading UN agency in the field of migration. Working closely with governmental, intergovernmental and non-governmental partners, IOM promotes humane and orderly migration for the benefit of all. It saves lives and protects people on the move, drives solutions to displacement, and facilitates pathways for regular migration, while providing services and advice to governments and migrants.
IOM is committed to fostering a respectful, inclusive and supportive workplace where all employees can thrive professionally and feel valued. By creating such an environment, IOM aims to better harness the full potential of migration and strengthen its support to people on the move.
IOM invites candidates from diverse backgrounds to apply and provides reasonable accommodation throughout the recruitment process when required. Learn more about IOM’s workplace culture at IOM workplace culture | International Organization for Migration

Required Qualifications and Experience
Education

- Master’s degree in Social Science, International Relations, Regional Studies or a related field from an accredited academic institution with two years of relevant professional experience; or,
- Bachelor’s degree in the above fields with four years of relevant professional experience.

Accredited Universities are those listed in the UNESCO World Higher Education Database.
Experience

- Experience working on protection mainstreaming issues in relation to programming in resettlement and complementary pathways, migration, United Nations, or international organization contexts is essential;
- Experience developing guidance and training, and experience giving training, is required;
- Experience in the USRAP, and particularly in activities requiring interaction with beneficiaries, is highly preferrable;
- Experience speaking publicly on protection mainstreaming issues is an advantage.

Skills

- Knowledge of current issues in the refugee context related to the protection of, and assistance to, a variety of populations is an advantage;
- A demonstrated background and knowledge of issues related to migration, preferably in the international context, is essential;
- Demonstrated proficiency with Stabilizing At-Risk Communities and Enhancing Migration Management to Enable Smooth Transitions (START) is an advantage;
- Excellent computer skills and a high level of proficiency in spreadsheet and database applications;
- Demonstrated knowledge of protection mainstreaming principles, including safety, dignity, accessibility, accountability, and beneficiary-centered approaches;
- Proven analytical skills, including the ability to assess risks, identify protection gaps, and translate findings into practical recommendations;
- Demonstrated training and facilitation skills, with the ability to deliver clear, practical, and engaging learning sessions to diverse audiences;
- Excellent communication skills, including preparation of interim, final, and ad‑hoc situation reports;
- Excellent organizational skills, including the ability to plan work, manage competing priorities, and meet deadlines;
- Ability to consolidate and analyze information from multiple sources and produce high‑quality reports for donors, partners, and internal stakeholders;
- Demonstrated interpersonal and coordination skills, including engagement with UN agencies, government counterparts, and implementing partners;
- High degree of judgment, initiative, and ability to work independently within assigned areas.

- Contribute to the development of a wide range of guidance, tools, and training materials that are specifically tailored for IOM personnel working on the USRAP through research, reviewing, editing, and writing. As needed, participate in monitoring and evaluation field visits.
- In coordination with the Senior Programme Coordinator, help guide and train relevant global networks of champions who support protection mainstreaming work within USRAP projects. - Serve as a resource for staff seeking advice on issues related to PSEA and follow established reporting requirements. Provide regular, effective PSEA training to all staff in the United States. Report as needed on PSEA work to the Global Office and IOM HQ.
