5. Project Context and Scope:
Background : The Global African Diaspora Summit (GADS), held in Sandton, Johannesburg, on Africa Day, 25 May 2012, adopted the GADS Declaration and Programme of Action, which remains a foundational framework for Africa’s engagement with its global diaspora. The Declaration set out commitments across three broad pillars of cooperation—political, economic and social—together with implementation and follow-up mechanisms. It also identified five legacy projects: the Skills Database of African Professionals; the African Diaspora Volunteer Corps; the African Diaspora Finance Corporation (ADFC) formerly the (African Diaspora Investment Fund) ; the Development Marketplace for the Diaspora (ADIMDMADA); and the African Institute for Remittances (AIR). More than a decade after the adoption of the Declaration, Africa’s role in the continental and global landscape has evolved significantly.
The African Union’s (AU) Agenda 2063 is being accelerated through its Second Ten-Year Implementation Plan, the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is operational, the African Union (AU) has taken its seat in the G20, and has entered a renewed phase of continental dialogue on reparatory justice. At the same time, the Declaration itself called for a rotational review every three or four years, supported by monitoring and evaluation mechanisms and adequate resourcing.

Accordingly, there is a need to take stock of progress made in implementing the commitments of the 2012 GADS Declaration, identify what has been achieved and what remains outstanding, and consider how the implementation agenda can be taken forward in light of the evolving continental and global geo-political context. The proposed virtual continental stocktaking consultation on the implementation of the 2012 GADS Declaration, which is tentatively planned to be conducted on the 23rd to 25th November 2026, therefore responds to this need in addition to reviewing the mechanism envisaged in the Declaration. It will provide an opportunity for AU Member States and other relevant stakeholders to take an honest and evidence-based stock of implementation progress, exchange experiences, identify good practices and lessons learned, and discuss practical and affordable approaches for strengthening diaspora engagement. The Consultation will also help prepare the ground for the 15-year commemoration of the GADS in 2027.

The Consultation builds on the processes that preceded and followed the 2012 Summit, including the three African Diaspora Ministerial Conferences held in Sandton in 2007, New York in 2011 and Pretoria in 2012; Technical Experts meetings; the Conferences of Intellectuals of Africa and the Diaspora; regional consultative conferences; and the biannual meetings of diaspora focal points. It also builds on ongoing programming and partnerships, including the AUC–IOM–AfDB joint diaspora-engagement initiative in eight transition states—The Gambia, Liberia, Madagascar, Mali, Somalia, South Sudan, Togo and Zimbabwe. The stocktaking will further take into account relevant global and continental initiatives that have contributed to advancing diaspora engagement. These include the International Conference on the Future Agenda of Action for Global Diaspora Engagement: Concrete Steps, Impactful Solutions, which supported the formalization and operationalization of the Global Diaspora Alliance, endorsed by several AU Member States as an outcome of the Dublin Declaration with the support of the African Union. The exercise will also build on the Trans-Continental Comparative Review of African Diaspora and Migrant Communities, undertaken in Djibouti in 2025.

Against this background, the African Union Commission, through AU-CIDO and the International Organization for Migration, Special Liaison Office (IOM-SLO) in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, is preparing to jointly organize a Three- Day Continental Stocktaking Consultation. The Consultation will seek to:

- Conduct a comprehensive stocktaking of the implementation status of the 2012 GADS Declaration and Programme of Action, including an assessment of progress under each of the three pillars and the status of the identified legacy projects.

- Exchange experiences and review national and regional diaspora-engagement plans and approaches.

- Identify good practices, lessons learned and innovative, affordable approaches that can inform future diaspora engagement efforts.

- Contribute to harmonizing the continental approach to diaspora engagement, while respecting nationally owned strategies, policies and priorities in line with the principle of subsidiarity; and

- Agree on preliminary outcomes and a forward-looking roadmap to sustain dialogue, follow-up and future consultations on the implementation of the GADS Declaration.

To support this process, AU-CIDO and IOM SLO intend to engage a consultant for approximately 6–8 weeks , over an approximate three-month period, to undertake the stocktaking exercise and a report outlining the achievements, bottlenecks and proposed way forward. Beyond documenting progress in implementing the 2012 Global African Diaspora Summit Declaration and Programme of Action, the assignment will identify strategic priorities, actionable recommendations, and opportunities for translating the stocktaking findings into future programming and policy interventions. This will include identifying gaps that require further attention, areas for strengthened collaboration among the African Union, Member States, and relevant stakeholders, and potential entry points for advancing the Declaration’s objectives.
In this regard, the consultant will be responsible for gathering and analyzing information from Member States and relevant stakeholders, documenting implementation progress and experiences, identifying good practices and lessons learned, and outstanding challenges; formulating practical, forward-looking recommendations to inform future initiatives and programming; preparing and presenting the stocktaking report, supporting the facilitation of the virtual Consultation, and incorporating feedback from the Consultation into the final stocktaking report. The Consultant will submit both the final revised stocktaking and workshop reports.
The Consultation is also expected to facilitate the Three-Day Stocktaking Consultations by leading the discussions, using agreed probing questions to guide the sessions in accordance with the identified themes and keeping the interventions focused on the subject matter.

Scope of Work and Key Tasks
Under the overall guidance of AU-CIDO and IOM SLO, the consultant will undertake the following tasks. The assignment will be implemented primarily following the virtual Continental Stocktaking Consultation, with limited preparatory engagement prior to the event. The pre-consultation phase will focus on introducing the exercise to Member State focal points, while the substantive stocktaking, analysis, report preparation and follow-up activities will be undertaken after the Consultation.

- Conduct preliminary virtual introductory meetings with the designated focal points of AU Member States to brief them on the objectives, scope and intended approach of the stocktaking exercise, as well as the purpose and expected outcomes of the upcoming virtual Continental Stocktaking Consultation.

- Explain the anticipated role of Member State focal points in facilitating the subsequent information-gathering process and contributing to the stocktaking exercise.

- Document the preliminary engagement with Member State focal points by submitting a brief summary of the introductory meetings conducted, including the Member States and focal points engaged, dates of meetings, key information communicated, and any initial feedback or considerations raised.

- With guidance from AU-CIDO, facilitate the virtual Continental Stocktaking Consultation, including the development of introductory presentations, discussion questions and other relevant materials, in coordination with AU-CIDO and IOM SLO.

- Support the presentation of the objectives, scope and proposed approach of the stocktaking exercise during the Consultation, as appropriate.

- Facilitate discussions to capture Member States’ and stakeholders’ perspectives on the implementation of the 2012 GADS Declaration and Programme of Action, including key achievements, challenges, lessons learned, and priorities for future action, to be further substantiated through comprehensive information gathering and validation phase, following the consultation.

- Document the key discussions, recommendations and proposed follow-up actions emerging from the Consultation to inform the subsequent information-gathering, analysis and preparation of the stocktaking report.

C. Information Gathering and Consultations

Following the virtual Continental Stocktaking Consultation, the consultant will:

- Conduct a thorough desk review of available information on the implementation of the 2012 GADS Declaration and Programme of Action.

- Gather and validate information from AU Member States through questionnaires, virtual interviews, targeted consultations and other appropriate means, building on the preliminary engagement with Member State focal points and inputs received during the Consultation.

- Consult relevant AU institutions, Regional Economic Communities (RECs), diaspora-related institutions and other stakeholders, as appropriate.

- Identify and document achievements, institutional and policy developments, good practices, innovative approaches, implementation challenges, gaps and lessons learned.

- Consolidate and analyse the information collected to assess implementation progress across the Declaration’s political, economic and social dimensions, as well as areas requiring further attention and action.

D. Preparation of the Continental Stocktaking Report and Forward-Looking Recommendations

- Prepare a draft Continental Stocktaking Report outlining progress in implementing the 2012 GADS Declaration and Programme of Action, key achievements, institutional and policy developments, good practices, challenges and gaps, and lessons learned.

- Develop practical, forward-looking recommendations identifying strategic priorities, opportunities for strengthened continental and national collaboration, and potential entry points for translating the stocktaking findings into future policies, initiatives and programming.

- Identify outstanding commitments and areas requiring follow-up, including possible actions by AU-CIDO, AU Member States, RECs and other relevant stakeholders.

- Present the draft findings and recommendations to AU-CIDO and IOM SLO for review and validation, and revise the report based on feedback received.

E. Finalization and Reporting

- Incorporate the feedback and inputs received during the Consultation, as well as findings from the subsequent information-gathering and analysis, into the Continental Stocktaking Report.

- Finalize the Continental Stocktaking Report, ensuring that it provides both a consolidated account of implementation progress and actionable recommendations to inform future policy development, programming and follow-up initiatives.

- Prepare a concise Workshop/Consultation Report capturing the objectives, proceedings, key discussions, stakeholder perspectives, recommendations and proposed follow-up actions.

- Submit the final revised Continental Stocktaking Report and the final Workshop/Consultation Report to AU-CIDO and IOM SLO for review and approval.

- Undertake any other closely related tasks necessary to complete the assignment, as agreed with AU-CIDO and IOM SLO.
