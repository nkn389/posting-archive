Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security. UN Women in Georgia supports state and non-state partners towards the achievement of substantive gender equality in Georgia. In line with national and international commitments, UN Women works on the levels of policy and legislation, institutions and grassroots, in order to achieve transformative results for increased gender equality and greater protection of the rights of women and girls. UN Women puts special emphasis on the work towards gender mainstreaming in good governance reforms and enhancing women’s political participation, ending violence against women, promoting and supporting women’s economic empowerment, and Women, Peace and Security agenda. Along with these key priority areas that constitute UN Women’s mandate, UN Women interventions in Georgia are anchored in the United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) in Georgia.

Progress towards gender equality remains slow and uneven, while resistance to women’s rights continues to grow globally. The UN Secretary-General’s report Women’s Rights in Review 30 Years After Beijing[1] highlights that nearly one-quarter of governments worldwide reported a backlash against women’s rights in 2024. Similarly, The Gender Snapshot 2025[2], produced by UN Women and the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (UN DESA), estimates that, at the current pace, gender parity in managerial positions will take nearly a century to achieve. These trends underscore the need for renewed efforts to challenge entrenched gender norms and accelerate progress towards equality. Engaging young people is critical to these efforts, as they can challenge stereotypes, shape public attitudes and drive social change. In Georgia, research suggests that younger generations generally hold more egalitarian attitudes than older groups on several aspects of gender equality. However, this progress cannot be taken for granted: traditional gender norms persist, while international evidence points to growing polarization among young people, particularly between women and men. Investing in youth engagement, critical thinking and leadership is therefore essential to equip young people with the knowledge, skills and confidence to challenge discrimination and advance gender equality.

Against this backdrop and with the support of the Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation (Norad), UN Women will be developing and conducting the Generation Equality Youth Lab. The Generation Equality Youth Lab is a flagship UN Women initiative designed as a five-day programme bringing together young people to explore gender equality, understand inequalities and discrimination, and examine the links between women’s empowerment and broader political, economic and social development. Through interactive learning, critical reflection, storytelling, debates and creative exercises, participants will explore how social norms, language, media and digital spaces shape attitudes and behaviours. The Lab will strengthen their critical thinking, communication and leadership skills, equipping them to challenge discriminatory attitudes, engage constructively with diverse perspectives and promote equality in their everyday environments. Ultimately, the initiative aims to cultivate a new generation of informed and confident gender equality champions who can mobilize their peers, build alliances and drive positive change in their communities and beyond.

To support the development of the Generation Equality Youth Lab curricula, UN Women intends to engage a National Consultant. The National Consultant will report to the UN Women Programme Specialist and UN Women Project Analyst and will be supported by the UN Women Project Assistant, who will be the point of contact for contractual and payment matters. In close coordination and under the overall guidance of UN Women, the consultant will undertake the tasks outlined below.

Description of Responsibilities/ Scope of Work

- Develop a comprehensive five-day Generation Equality Youth Lab curriculum, informed by global best practices, innovative learning methodologies and consultations with UN Women. The curriculum should translate the initiative’s thematic priorities into a coherent learning experience, with clearly defined learning objectives, thematic sessions and participatory methodologies tailored to young people.

- Ensure the curriculum is youth-centered, engaging and innovative, moving beyond conventional training formats dominated by lectures and presentations. The programme should prioritize interactive, experiential and creative learning approaches that reflect young people’s interests, encourage active participation and create an engaging environment for critical thinking, dialogue and collaborative problem-solving.

- Develop a programme structure and session plans for each of the five days, outlining key topics, learning objectives, facilitation approaches, interactive activities, estimated duration and expected learning outcomes.

- Integrate participatory and experiential learning approaches, including critical reflection, storytelling, group discussions, debates, role-play, case studies and creative exercises, to encourage active engagement, peer learning and the practical application of knowledge.

- Define measurable learning outcomes and assessment methods, including approaches to assess participants’ knowledge, skills and attitudes at the beginning and end of the programme, as well as opportunities for reflection on how participants can apply their learning in their communities.

- Integrate a practical component on youth-led initiatives and community action planning, enabling participants to identify opportunities to apply their learning, mobilize their peers and develop ideas for small-scale initiatives that promote gender equality in their communities.

- Consult with UN Women throughout the development process to review and refine the proposed programme structure, thematic content and ensuring alignment with the objectives and intended results of the Generation Equality Youth Lab.

- Submit a final, consolidated curriculum package incorporating UN Women’s feedback and ready for implementation.
